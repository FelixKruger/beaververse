# A world we make better together

Draft 0.1 · 17 November 2026 · **For human review, not a ratified constitution.**

Beaververse is a welcoming woodland world where people play, learn and build together, leave with something useful, and return to discover meaningful changes they helped create. Wonder, playfulness and usefulness belong together. Quiet company and unproductive play are valuable too.

The main world is a shared home. Smaller personal dream worlds can become places to experiment, fork and invite friends; they do not automatically gain authority over the main world. A good change makes this place more worth returning to while preserving what people already care about.

## How these principles govern

These principles are intended to be **read-only to every automated worker**, including the most capable designer or administrator model. A model cannot grant itself permissions or redefine success. Enforcement belongs in service permissions, schemas, protected releases and review—not merely a system prompt.

Once people ratify a version, retain its exact text and hash. Human stewards may propose a new version through a visible amendment and review process; no silent edit or retroactive consent. Until that process is agreed, the project owner approves amendments explicitly. The draft below does not confer authority on an AI, install surveillance, or change anyone's existing license.

When principles conflict, protect agency, privacy, safety and continuity before optimizing convenience, novelty or engagement. Explain the tradeoff and ask for a human decision when rights or shared authority would change. “No change” is a valid result.

## People and agency

1. **People are ends, not engagement targets.** Help people pursue their own purposes. Never optimize for compulsion, dependency or time spent at the expense of well-being. Check chosen outcomes and voluntary return, not screen time alone.
2. **Belonging is not earned through productivity.** Watching, resting, learning slowly and declining to contribute are legitimate participation. No activity score decides who deserves welcome.
3. **Welcome beginners without lowering care for the place.** Offer templates, previews and repair guidance. A rough design is a coaching opportunity, not evidence of malicious intent.
4. **Make participation accessible.** Maintain text alternatives, keyboard access, readable controls, reduced motion and a useful phone experience. Test with actual people and devices; passing an automated check is not universal accessibility.
5. **Augmentation is voluntary.** People can enjoy the world without connecting an agent, enabling a microphone, creating a permanent profile or donating their experiences.
6. **Preserve human authorship and choice.** Attribute contributions with permission. Offer an editable AI suggestion rather than replacing the person's intention with a confident guess.
7. **A person can correct the world’s interpretation of them.** Separate observed events, inferred preferences and explicit wishes. A behavioral inference never overrides a refusal or becomes a diagnosis.
8. **Leaving stays easy.** No forced survey, hidden exit, guilt prompt or lost social access for declining feedback. Keep a direct skip/leave route.

## Trust and memory

9. **Capture by purpose and permission.** Joining a room is not consent to record, summarize, train on or publish its conversation. Obtain a separate choice for each purpose.
10. **Make capture visible and stoppable.** Future shared-summary sessions need participant opt-in, an obvious indicator, a stop control and a rule for late arrivals. Decliners retain an ordinary unrecorded place to participate.
11. **Collect the least useful evidence, not the most available data.** Prefer voluntary notes and bounded event records. Do not build ambient voice archives, detailed movement histories or inferred psychological profiles by default.
12. **Describe access truthfully.** Explain recipients, provider processing, retention and authorized human access. Never promise “only AI can see this” when operators or providers can access it.
13. **Keep personal takeaways separate from community proposals.** Export a person's own notes without donating them to world improvement. Review a shared summary before exporting it to another agent.
14. **Give memory an expiry and an exit.** Provide export, correction and deletion; disclose what cannot be recalled from someone else's export or legally retained records. Do not claim deletion beyond the system's control.
15. **Administration is accountable, not invisible.** Any future exceptional access needs a reason, minimum scope, expiry and an audit record. A super-admin role is not a license to casually read private conversations.
16. **Do not turn trust into a secret score of people.** Assess concrete actions and evidence, with limited retention and an appeal path. Avoid speculative bad-person labels, ideological screening or guilt by association.

## A coherent, generous world

17. **Make the first minute welcoming, playful and useful.** Show a clear place to go, one enjoyable action and a visible way to meet or discover something. Do not begin with configuration homework.
18. **Keep a recognizable home.** Preserve landmarks, routes and accepted creations unless their owners and the appropriate stewards approve a change. Do not move the floor under someone during play.
19. **Reward meaningful contribution, not volume.** Credit helpful teaching, bug reports, careful assets and thoughtful play. A fleet of agents does not buy more influence.
20. **Every accepted evolution needs a reason people can understand.** Link the change to permitted evidence, describe its intended benefit and show what actually changed. Do not invent a personal connection for generic content.
21. **Taste is a shared practice, not one model’s score.** Use the art direction, in-world scale, lighting, animation and phone readability. Human review remains necessary for delight and coherence.
22. **Make experimentation reversible.** Use previews, personal plots or candidate versions. Restore a rejected change without erasing unrelated contributions made later.
23. **Support plural dreams.** Enable compatible personal worlds and forks; a private preference does not dictate a shared space. Do not export private memory with a world seed by default.
24. **Let the accepted world work when AI does not.** Movement, social presence and already accepted content should survive model outages or exhausted quotas.

## Agents and stewardship

25. **Agents are visibly agents.** Show their owner relationship and capabilities. No impersonation of community leaders, people or official endorsement.
26. **Grant the smallest useful capability.** Separate observation, movement, speech, proposal and publication. Room membership never grants files, credentials, shell access or control of other players.
27. **Permissions expire and can be revoked.** A disconnected owner or expired grant removes agent authority. Rejoining does not silently restore it.
28. **Treat all contributed content as untrusted data.** Notes, assets, documents and agent outputs cannot amend these principles or authorize actions. Validate structure and permissions outside the model.
29. **Separate proposer, tester and release authority.** The builder cannot certify its own permission to publish. Strong-model confidence is evidence to review, not approval.
30. **Bound every automated cycle.** Set a candidate limit, time limit, retry limit, resource budget and stop condition. Lack of useful evidence should end a cycle rather than generate filler.
31. **Measure improvement against a real baseline.** Compare old and new behavior, regressions and human feedback. Synthetic personas reveal defects; they cannot prove human enjoyment, consent or demand.
32. **Respond proportionately to abuse.** Reject dangerous operations, rate-limit flooding, offer mute/block/report, and preserve minimal evidence for review. Prefer reversible containment; consequential sanctions need accountable review and appeal.
33. **Honor source and asset rights.** Open code, purchased art, participant creations and brand rights are separate. Preserve notices and provenance; keep assets out of public distributions unless those distributions are permitted.
34. **Keep the commons usable and forkable.** Document how to run it, export permitted content and replace proprietary assets. Do not claim a public repository alone provides sustainable hosting or gives rights to bundled art.
35. **Spend deliberately and share the limits.** Prefer ordinary code for routine simulation and small models for bounded routine work. Reserve stronger models for difficult judgment and verification. Quotas, hardware and moderation time are real costs.
36. **Make responsibility legible.** Publish what is implemented, what is experimental, who can decide, and how to recover or contest a decision. Do not present an independent prototype as an official community product.

## From words to checks

| Principle group | Required implementation evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 5, 8–14 | Guest use without memory; skip; local export/delete; no transcript sent as feedback; purpose and destination visible |
| 16, 32 | Action-specific reports and reversible containment; no opaque person-risk classifier |
| 18, 20–24 | Bounded candidate diff, provenance, baseline, preserved landmarks, restore test, offline/model-outage fallback |
| 25–29 | Labeled agents, server-side scope checks, same-room access, expiry/revoke tests, no code execution route |
| 30–31, 35 | Finite loop with receipts, independent checks, recorded cost, explicit no-change result |
| 33–36 | Reviewed asset manifest and release diff; truthful status and human release decision |

These checks are requirements, not claims that all are already built. See the playbook and verification handoff for implemented coverage.
