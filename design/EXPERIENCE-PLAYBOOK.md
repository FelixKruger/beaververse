# Beaververse: a workshop you want to return to

Working experience direction, 18 November 2026. These are review criteria, not changes to the protected governance principles or proof of player retention.

## The promise

Walk into a woodland workshop, find people worth building with, and leave with something useful of your own. Return because a person, a project or a shared place matters to you.

The village is the interface: the arrival path invites exploration; the fire welcomes conversation; the workshop supports asking and showing; the garden lets visitors leave a small shared mark. A panel should help finish an action in the world, then give the world back.

## Twelve experience principles

1. **Give the place a purpose.** Each new landmark should support meeting, making, learning, resting or discovering. Reject prop clutter that only fills empty ground.
2. **Let the player choose their pace.** Wandering quietly, watching and leaving are valid. No forced icebreaker, microphone, account or agent.
3. **Make the beaver feel embodied.** Turning, contact, stride, hands and greeting must fit this character. Test animation on the actual rig at actual travel speed.
4. **Make helping easy to start.** A useful prompt asks for one obstacle or one thing to show. It opens a draft; the visitor chooses what to share.
5. **Reward with consequences people can see.** A contribution should produce a comprehensible shared change. Celebration follows a real result and does not interrupt control.
6. **Carry a thread across visits.** Help visitors recover what they deliberately saved and choose what to do next. Never fabricate memory or imply surveillance.
7. **Let surprise grow from continuity.** Keep recognizable paths and meeting places while introducing reviewed discoveries. Random regeneration must not erase belonging.
8. **Make other people legible.** Show who is present, which room they share, and how to approach or leave. Never simulate people or crowd activity to hide an empty room.
9. **Be useful when the room is quiet.** Let a lone visitor explore, prepare a question, try a small creation and keep an idea without requiring a fake conversation.
10. **Keep assistance subordinate to people.** Clearly identify AI companions. Capability grants are specific, revocable and enforced outside the model; a conversation is not permission to execute.
11. **Let people leave well.** Saving, exporting and feedback are optional. No streak loss, guilt, blocked exits or automatic messages to lure someone back.
12. **Judge the whole visit.** A good screenshot, test count or menu inventory cannot establish that arriving, meeting, doing and returning form a coherent experience.

## First visit and return: a testable arc

| Moment | Player outcome | Existing support | Next gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrive | I understand where I am and can move comfortably | Guest name, woodland view, keyboard/touch controls, Left Shift run | First-time comprehension and physical-phone testing |
| Approach | I can find a place and a person without embarrassment | Room presence, text, optional voice, wave, raised workshop | Participant mute/report and a better social arrival cue |
| Do | We exchange one useful thing or make a small shared change | Workshop drafts, shared visit garden, scoped companions | A coherent shared activity with completion and credit |
| Leave | I can take something with me without surrendering private conversation | Explicit local takeaways and separate feedback export | Optional next-action structure, without another compulsory form |
| Return | I can pick up a thought and recognize what changed | Latest saved takeaway can be deliberately continued | Persistent reviewed contributions and a truthful “since your last visit” view |

Garden objects currently disappear when their visitor leaves. Local notes expire after 30 days and belong to the browser, not an account. These limits must stay visible. A private note card improves continuity; it does not implement the persistent evolving-world vision by itself.

## Persona review and playtest

Use separate real people when possible. These scenarios are design tests, not invented user findings.

- **Curious newcomer:** enter without an account, find the workshop, explain one useful thing to do. Watch for wandering without purpose and unexplained controls.
- **Quiet learner:** decline microphone and all capture, remain welcome, find a prompt, save or leave without friction. Watch for social pressure.
- **Returning builder:** save one authentic takeaway, close the page, come back, recognize it and continue without losing a newer draft. Ask whether it was actually useful.
- **Host and neighbor:** meet in the same room, exchange a project question and verify the neighbor received it. Test room separation and leaving.
- **Agent owner:** connect a bounded companion, demonstrate denied ungranted actions, revoke it, then continue playing. Copy-paste ease must not obscure authority.
- **Phone visitor:** reach the workshop and controls on a real target phone with representative room load. An emulated viewport is not a device-performance result.

Invite 4–6 people to two short sessions. Ask them to bring one real small project/question. In session two ask what they remember, whether any interaction helped, what they came back for and whether they can resume it. Record only consented observations. Do not optimize minutes spent or infer enjoyment from dwell time. Sample size is qualitative; it cannot establish population retention.

## Quality gate for each iteration

State the desired player feeling and behavior; choose the smallest mechanism that could create it; observe the whole route; record surprises/failures; keep or revert. For every added asset check source rights, palette, silhouette, contact/collision, camera readability, payload and frame time. A new download is not automatically a graphics improvement.

Before claiming meaningful self-evolution, demonstrate a real suggestion → reviewed proposal → reversible shared change → visible result on a later visit. Preserve author attribution and moderation, with no privileged execution from untrusted notes. Before public launch, close moderation, hosting, recovery, phone performance and licensed public-avatar gaps.

## Game-design research used as lenses

The [MDA framework by Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek](https://www.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/pubs/MDA.pdf) links mechanics, the dynamics they produce and player experience. Our application is to judge an asset or feature by the social/discovery behavior it supports, rather than its presence in a checklist.

[Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski's 2006 study](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf) examines autonomy, competence and relatedness as predictors of enjoyment and future play. Our inference is to prioritize freely chosen activity, understandable progress and meaningful connection. This research does not establish that Beaververse already produces those outcomes.
